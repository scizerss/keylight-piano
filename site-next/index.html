Your own music

Bring any song.
Even an MP3.

Drop a recording on the window. Keylight listens to it, writes the notes, and turns it into a lesson with the original track playing along.

  1. 1

    Drop a file

    MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC, AAC, OGG or AIFF, up to twenty minutes. MIDI and MusicXML files work too.

  2. 2

    Keylight listens

    The notes are worked out on your computer. Your file is never uploaded. Want it easier? Keep just the melody and a bass line.

  3. 3

    Play along

    The recording plays in sync and keeps its pitch when you slow it down. A wrong note? Fix it in the editor.

It becomes a song like any other.

  • Wait mode, half speed, loops and both hands
  • Cover art and lyrics, found for you on Mac and Windows
  • Drop a lyrics file on a song to sing along
  • Sheet music for MusicXML files

Works best with piano or solo recordings. Links to streaming sites are not supported.

Sheet music

Read it, with the lights on.

Switch to Sheet only and the falling notes give way to the score. The line shows where you are, the note that is due glows in the colour of its hand, and your keys still light the way.

The course

From middle C to real music.

Twelve levels of short lessons, each ending in something to play. Go in order, or open anything you like: the course suggests, it never locks.

Supporting Keylight

Free, and staying free.

On Mac, Windows and Android nothing is locked, and nothing will be. Keylight is made by one person in spare time. If it has earned a place on your desk, a tip helps it keep moving.

What a tip pays for

The tools used to build and test each release, and keyboards to add support for. More devices is the most asked-for thing, and each one has to be on a desk to get it right.

iPhone and iPad: a paid App Store app

The app already runs on them. The App Store version will be a paid app, which covers Apple's yearly developer fee and keeps the work going. Mac, Windows and Android stay free, and the code is open, so you can also build the iPhone version yourself.

Other ways to help

Tell someone with a light-up keyboard. Send a device report if yours is not supported yet. Report what breaks. All of it counts as much as money.

Questions

Good to know.

Which keyboards does it work with?
The ROLI Piano M and LUMI Keys over USB, with every key lighting up. The 49-key ROLI Piano and two keyboards joined as one work in beta. On Android, Bluetooth keyboards work too.
I used to learn with ROLI Learn. Is this the same?
Keylight is an independent, free app for the same keyboards that does a similar job: falling notes, lit keys, lessons and your own songs, on your computer or phone. It is not made by ROLI and is not affiliated with or endorsed by ROLI.
Does it need an account or the internet?
No. There is no account, and everything works offline. Your songs and progress stay on your device.
Is it really free?
Yes, and nothing is locked. It is open source under the MIT licence. Tips are welcome but never required.
Can I learn without a light-up keyboard?
You can look around the app without a keyboard, but the lit keys need a keyboard that lights up.
