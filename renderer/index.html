    Hand tracking beta

    Point a camera down at the keyboard (an iPhone in Desk View works well). Keylight then shows which finger is over which key, before you press it.

    Uses MIDI note presses and fresh camera observations. Uncertain fingers are not judged, and this does not change your note score. The camera covers one 24-key keyboard; the experimental Airwave source is calibrated on the keys on screen.

    Let’s set up your keyboard

    Three quick checks, then your first lesson.

    Later, in the player, − and + at the top right move your keys an octave down or up. Z and X do the same.

    Your keys can light up and follow the music. Optional — takes about 2 minutes.

    Show install steps

    To install it you need Homebrew (brew.sh). Paste these two lines into Terminal, then quit and reopen Keylight:

    brew install uv python@3.13
uv tool install --python /opt/homebrew/bin/python3.13 --from "git+https://github.com/hyperb1iss/blocksd@v0.6.0" blocksd

        Keep Keylight growing
        
  
        Keylight is free and open source. Every feature is available without paying, and the app works offline without an account.
        
  
        Keylight is made by one person in spare time. Building it costs real money: the tools used to write and test it, and keyboards to add support for. If it has helped you, a tip on Ko-fi, once or monthly, keeps that going.
        
  
        Keylight stays free on Mac, Windows and Android. The iPhone and iPad version will be a paid app on the App Store, which covers Apple's yearly developer fee.
        
  
        Ko-fi opens in your browser. Keylight does not collect payment details or check whether you have donated.
